# Policies and support

These are the course-wide rules, in one place so the assessment briefs can point here instead of repeating themselves. Where a rule had a choice to make, the reasoning is written next to it — this is a course about what decisions cost, and it would be a poor advertisement for that if its own rules arrived as bare assertions.

## Late work

Work submitted late loses 5% of the available marks per day, counted in whole days, to a floor of 50%. After ten days it is no longer accepted, because feedback that arrives after the next assessment is already due is not feedback.

The penalty escalates rather than sitting flat, for the same reason a retry backs off: a fixed penalty makes the second day cost exactly what the first did, so there is no pressure to stop. What it trades is a small unfairness at the boundary — a submission eleven minutes late and one twenty-three hours late are charged identically. That is a deliberate choice of a rule you can compute yourself over one you would have to ask about.

## Extensions

Ask before the deadline. An extension requested in advance is granted for any reason a reasonable person would accept, in writing, usually within a day. An extension requested after the deadline has passed needs documentation, and is a different and slower process.

The asymmetry is the whole policy, and it is week 9 applied to ourselves: an answer that arrives after the person needed it is not worth what it cost to produce. Asking beforehand is cheap for you and cheap for us. Asking afterwards means reconstructing what happened, which is expensive for both of us and worse at getting you what you need.

## Resubmission

You have two resubmissions for the semester, not two per assessment. Spend them how you like: both on the retry audit, one each on the audit and the policy, or neither.

This is a retry budget, and it is a budget for the same reason week 5 argues for one. A per-assessment allowance is a per-layer count — it looks modest at each point and multiplies across the course, and it also spends most of its attempts on the assessment you understood best. A shared budget makes you decide where a second attempt is actually worth having.

Some work cannot be resubmitted into a pass, and we will tell you that rather than let you spend a resubmission finding out. If a submission missed the brief instead of executing it imperfectly, the useful next step is a conversation, not another attempt.

## Academic integrity and the use of AI

The University's academic integrity rules apply in full, and the short version is that the work has to be yours and any assistance has to be declared.

Generative AI is permitted throughout this course, on one condition: you have to be able to defend what you submit. The week 8 assessment is explicitly a defence in front of a panel, and the week 12 assessment is an argument against someone briefed to take the other side. Neither of those goes well for work you cannot explain. Declare what you used and what for, in a short note with the submission — the declaration is not marked, and its absence is treated as a matter of integrity rather than a formatting slip.

## Getting help, and who to ask

Ask [Idris Fenn](/people/idris-fenn/) first for anything with code or a stack trace in it, and bring the failing thing rather than a description of it. Ask [Marisol Quaye](/people/marisol-quaye/) for anything about what the course is doing or what an assessment is really asking for.

If that does not resolve it, or if the problem is with one of us, contact the School of Invented Disciplines' student administration, who will route it to someone who is not us.

For anything affecting your ability to study — illness, a crisis, access requirements — contact the University's student support services directly. You do not need to disclose anything to us to get an extension; the request itself is enough.
