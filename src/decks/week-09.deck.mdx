# Retrying into an empty room

Week 9 — SLOP3092

<a href={withBase("/lectures/week-09/")}>&larr; Back to the lecture page</a>

---

## Last week

A circuit breaker, open and half-open, refusing calls on the evidence of earlier ones.

Every caller stopped rediscovering the same outage separately.

---

## This week: the caller stopped waiting

A user taps a button, waits three seconds, sees nothing, and closes the tab.

The request is still being retried.

---

## The reflex

The policy counts attempts and nothing else.

Nothing in it asks whether anybody is still waiting for the answer, and nothing in the call stack has been told the connection is gone.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# 30s × 3 = 90s of work for nobody

---

## What those ninety seconds hold

A worker thread, a connection slot, a row lock. The answer, when it finally arrives, gets written to a closed socket.

Under load this compounds: abandoned attempts queue ahead of live requests and hold their resources longer than live ones do, so the share of capacity working for nobody grows.

---

## The fix

An absolute deadline — a timestamp, not a duration — propagated through the call and checked before each attempt and each inner call.

A call entered with 300 ms left on the clock gets 300 ms, not a fresh 30 seconds.

gRPC and Go's `context.Context` carry one by default. Most HTTP clients do not.

---

## What a deadline cancels

Work that was about to succeed.

A deadline set 200 ms too tight fails requests the user would happily have waited for. And one layer that starts a fresh timeout instead of honouring the one it was handed restores the old behaviour beneath it, silently.

---

## Who decides

`client`.

Weeks 1 and 2 asked the client whether a retry was safe. This asks whether it is still wanted, and the caller is the only party that knows.

A library sees attempt counts. The platform sees load. Neither can see a closed tab.

---

## In the lab

1. Log the time left on the caller's deadline at each attempt, and watch attempts continue past zero
2. Add propagation and recount them
3. Then set the deadline 200 ms too tight and count the requests it kills
