# Counting to twenty-seven

Week 5 — SLOP3092

<a href={withBase("/lectures/week-05/")}>&larr; Back to the lecture page</a>

---

## Last week

Ten thousand clients, each backing off correctly on its own.

All retried on the same second anyway.

Each one was correct in isolation, and together they were a spike.

---

## This week: same shape, one request

A request passes through three services stacked on top of each other.

The innermost one starts failing intermittently.

---

## The reflex

Let each layer retry the call below it up to three times.

Three retries, at any one layer, seems modest.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# 3 × 3 × 3 = ?

---

## Twenty-seven

Twenty-seven requests reach the failing dependency, for the one request the user made.

The retries compound multiplicatively up the stack. They do not add.

---

## Nobody can see it happening

Each layer only sees its own three attempts.

The total is only visible from a vantage point no single layer has.

---

## The fix

One retry budget, shared by the whole call chain.

Held as a fraction of recent calls rather than a count per layer, so retries throttle themselves as the ratio nears the cap.

---

## What a shared budget refuses

A layer that would have recovered on its second attempt is sometimes refused it, because another layer drew the budget down first.

The reason a call wasn't retried now lives somewhere the person debugging it isn't looking.

---

## Who decides

`platform`.

A per-layer count can only ever see its own three attempts. Keeping the total sane needs a budget the whole call chain draws from.

---

## In the lab

1. Trace a three-layer call stack with independent per-layer retries
2. Count the amplification by hand
3. Replace the per-layer counts with one shared retry budget
4. Confirm the total drops to three

Bring the amplified count to the first review.
