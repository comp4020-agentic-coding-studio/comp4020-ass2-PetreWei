# 10,000 well-behaved clients

---

## Why they all return on the same second

Ten thousand clients retrying on the same second is a thundering herd.

The recovering dependency is hit by a synchronised spike — and falls over again.

No single client did anything wrong.

---

## Backoff never said when

Week 3 fixed *how long* one client waits.

It says nothing about *when* that client waits, relative to the other nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine.

---

## The fix

Randomise each client's wait inside its own backoff window.

On every attempt — not only the first.

---

## What jitter takes away

Nothing a single client can feel, which is why it gets left out of the implementation.

What changes is that no client can state its own worst-case wait any more — only the shape of the distribution it draws from.

---

## Who decides

`platform`.

No one client can see the other nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine. The fix has to be a property the platform requires of every client; one client adding jitter on its own changes nothing about the spike.

---

## In the lab

1. Run a simulated recovery with ten synchronised clients
2. Watch it fail
3. Add jitter to each client's backoff, independently
4. Watch the same recovery succeed
