## The reflex

Each one is behaving well in isolation.

# 10,000 well-behaved clients

---

## What it costs

Ten thousand clients retrying on the same second is a thundering herd.

The recovering dependency is hit by a synchronised spike — and falls over again.

No single client did anything wrong.

---

## Correct alone. Wrong together.

Backoff fixed *how long* one client waits.

It never touched *when*, relative to everyone else.

---

## The fix

Randomise each client's wait inside its own backoff window.

On every attempt — not only the first.

---

## What it costs

Nothing a single client can feel, which is exactly why it gets left out.

The cost lands on whoever operates the fleet: no client can now state its own worst-case latency, only the shape of its distribution.

---

## Who decides

`platform`.

No one client can see the other nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine. The fix has to be a property the platform requires of every client, not a courtesy any one of them can opt into.

---

## In the lab

1. Run a simulated recovery with ten synchronised clients
2. Watch it fail
3. Add jitter to each client's backoff, independently
4. Watch the same recovery succeed
