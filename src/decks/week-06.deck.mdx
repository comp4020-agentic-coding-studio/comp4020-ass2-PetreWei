## This week: no code in the failure

A scheduled payout fails at 3am. Two people are paged for the same alert.

Neither knows the other is awake, and both can reach the button that resends it.

---

## The reflex

Resend it.

Resending it is what fixing this has always meant. The second person arrives four minutes later, sees a failed payout on the dashboard, and does the same.

# Two payouts leave the account

---

## The retry no system logged

No retry loop. No idempotency key to add. Nothing in an exception handler to change.

The shape is week 2 exactly — the second actor could not tell whether the work had already been done — except that the state lived on a dashboard and in a group chat rather than in a database.

---

## The fix

A runbook step naming one person as the only one who presses resend for the duration of the incident.

That person checks the payout log first, and says in the incident channel that they are about to press it.

---

## What the runbook does not enforce

This is a weaker guarantee than any of the code fixes so far.

A written step is slower than a button at 3am, and it holds only if a tired person reads it and follows it. There is no enforcement anywhere: it is an agreement between two people, and agreements fail differently from locks.

---

## Who decides

`operator`.

No client and no platform appear in this failure, because there is no code between the failure and the resend. The decision belongs to whoever is holding the pager.

Which is the argument for writing it down. The parts of a system made of people fail the same ways and get less attention.

---

## In the lab

1. Run the incident as a role-play: two people, one dashboard, one failed payout
2. No communication outside the incident channel
3. Write the runbook line that would have stopped the second resend
4. Hand it to a pair who have not seen the scenario and see whether it survives them
