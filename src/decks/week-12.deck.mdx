## Eleven weeks

Idempotency keys. Backoff. Jitter. Shared budgets. Breakers. Deadlines.

Every one of them bought something and spent something else.

---

## This week

A failure from earlier in the semester, handed back with the outcome removed.

Something failed. Somebody retried. The account stops before saying whether that helped.

---

## The reflex

Assume the retry was the villain.

Eleven weeks of this course have mostly been about retries going wrong.

---

## What it costs

A verdict reached without doing the work.

The reflex to distrust retrying can be just as unexamined as the reflex to trust it was, in week 1.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# There is no fix this week

---

## The trade is the assessment

Name what retrying bought.

Name what it cost, in the units the last eleven weeks have used.

Say which you would have chosen, knowing only what was knowable at the time.

---

## What it costs

The comfort of hedging.

You have to commit to a position you can be wrong about, in front of somebody assigned to argue the other side.

---

## Who decides

`product`.

Whether retrying was worth it is a call about what the business was willing to trade — latency against correctness, user experience against load. A product decision dressed up as a technical one, for eleven weeks.

---

## In the lab

1. Take the case
2. Argue for or against having retried
3. Name what you would measure to know if you were right
4. Defend the argument against a partner assigned to take the opposite side
