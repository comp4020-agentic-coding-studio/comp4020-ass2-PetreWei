## One question, twelve times

Something failed. Should you try again, and how?

Twelve weeks, twelve situations. This week's is the one where the answer is yes.

---

## A read that times out once

```text
GET /accounts/8814/balance
```

Thirty seconds, then a timeout. The endpoint is read-only, so nothing on the server changed.

---

## The reflex

Retry.

Nobody classifies the failure first. The call did not return, so the call gets made again.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# The second attempt returns 200

---

## Why it worked

Sending that request twice produces the same result as sending it once.

The property is idempotence, and here it holds for a dull reason: the endpoint only reads.

---

## The precondition nobody wrote down

The retry depended on a fact about this one endpoint, and the three lines of retry code do not mention it.

Write it down: **repeating this call cannot change anything on the server.**

---

## Change one word

```text
POST /charges
```

The retry code is unchanged. The sentence you just wrote down is now false.

That is next week.

---

## Who decides

`client`.

A loop and a counter, inline at the call site. No configuration, no policy, nothing to look up.

Note where it sits. By week 5 the same decision has moved two layers away and nobody can find it.

---

## In the lab

1. Point a client at a read endpoint rigged to time out on one request in four
2. Retry it
3. Watch it succeed

You will spend eleven weeks being asked to distrust this, so watch it work once.
