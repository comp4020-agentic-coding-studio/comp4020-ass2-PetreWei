# Watching a breaker trip

Week 8 — SLOP3092

<a href={withBase("/lectures/week-08/")}>&larr; Back to the lecture page</a>

---

## The situation

A dependency goes down — for minutes, not seconds.

Long enough that no per-request backoff is going to help.

---

## The reflex

Keep retrying each request against the dependency individually.

The same schedule that worked for a two-second blip in week 3.

---

## What it costs

Every request still pays the full timeout finding out the dependency is down.

A fact that was already known after the first failure.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# Closed. Open. Half-open.

---

## Closed

Requests flow through normally.

The breaker is counting failures.

---

## Open

Enough requests have failed.

The breaker stops sending requests immediately — no more timeouts spent finding out.

---

## Half-open

After a cooldown, exactly one probe is let through.

It asks whether the dependency is back. Closing again only if that probe succeeds.

---

## The step that actually matters

Skip half-open, and a breaker either never risks finding out the dependency has recovered, or floods it the instant it reopens.

That's week 3's problem again, delayed.

---

## What it costs

Availability it might have had.

While the breaker is open, requests fail that would have worked — a known small loss now, instead of an unknown larger one spread across every caller.

---

## Who decides

`platform`.

A circuit breaker is shared state the whole client needs to see. Not something any individual request can hold on its own.

---

## In the lab

1. Wire a breaker in front of a dependency seeded to fail for an extended window
2. Watch requests stop reaching it once the breaker opens
3. Confirm it closes again once the dependency recovers
4. Confirm what happens when it doesn't

This is the defence you'll give in this week's retry policy review.
